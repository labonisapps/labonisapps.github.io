Pacbedonis

Pacbedonis calculates electron diffraction and scanning transmission electron microscope images of perfect crystals by the multislice method. Run a calculation and change the conditions in your hand, at the instrument or on the way there.

Solve the exit wave once and CBED, PACBED and the STEM image all come out of it. The thickness series is recorded on the way through the same calculation, so it costs no extra time.

Not on Google Play yet. This page describes the app as it stands; the store link will appear here when it is published.

Step by step

1One calculation, three outputs

Pick a structure and start a calculation. One probe gives CBED; scanning the unit cell and averaging gives PACBED; integrating a detector angular range at each position gives the STEM image.

Calculations go through a queue, and what comes out is kept under saved results, so a long run does not have to be repeated to look at it again.

2Choose the structure

Si, SrTiO3, GaAs and Au are there as samples, or open a CIF of your own. A CIF with symmetry operations is used as-is; one with only a space-group symbol is expanded from that symbol.

What the app can handle is stated on the screen rather than buried: perfect crystals with unit-cell periodicity. Defects, interfaces and grain boundaries are out of scope.

3Or take one from the 56,000+ bundled

Search by name or formula, or pick elements on the periodic table to narrow the bundled list down. Star the ones you use often.

4Watch it change with thickness

The thickness series comes out of the same calculation, so a slider steps through it rather than re-running. These three are the same crystal at 50.4, 100.7 and 200.3 nm, all from one 24.9 second run.

The angular range and the thickness are printed above the slider, so the picture you are looking at always carries the conditions that made it.

5Thickness from your own PACBED

Load a PACBED you photographed and the app matches it against the calculated thickness series. Rotation, scale and a shifted centre are fitted automatically.

This matching is an experimental feature. It has only been verified against synthetic patterns; the centre offset, moire and non-linear brightness of a real capture are not accounted for. Treat the result as an indication.

6Pull a structure from the open database

Search by material name or formula. This is the only part of the app that uses the network.

Good to know

  • The probe is built from the aperture, defocus, spherical aberration, astigmatism and coma. The app measures the resulting intensity distribution and reports the probe diameter at half maximum, so you see what you actually made. A source size — the incoherent blur — can be included
  • STEM detectors: BF, ABF, HAADF, centre of mass, iDPC, 4-segment DPC, and custom ranges
  • 4D-STEM — the diffraction pattern at every probe position can be saved
  • Elastic scattering factors from Lobato & Van Dyck (2014), Z = 1 to 103
  • Thermal diffuse scattering is treated as an absorptive potential (Thomas, Cleverley & Beanland 2024). HAADF is taken from the absorption, because a coherent calculation never puts thermal diffuse scattering at high angles and integrating the exit wave underestimates HAADF by an order of magnitude

Your data

Everything is calculated on the device. The app has no account, collects no personal information, and sends nothing to us. Searching the Crystallography Open Database is the only time it reaches the network, and that request carries only your search words.
